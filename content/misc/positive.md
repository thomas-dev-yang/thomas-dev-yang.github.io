---
title: Inability to create
date: 2026-08-27
---

I can talk about how actually, Logical Positivism isn't actually closely
related to the scientific method. Even though you can ragebait
people by saying, "Well, logical positivism says that only mathematical truths
and scientific truths matter; what's wrong with that?", that's not correct as
a specific instantiation onto a century-old philosophy designed to also try
and solve other problems.

I could talk about how actually, practice-based philosophies, or even just
engaging with science / math properly is a better use of time.

I could talk about how Carnap and Quine had arguments, the issues with
Carnapian L-language architecture.

None of this matters to an actual scientist or mathematician though. Even if
the conclusion to all this philosophy is discussing the very meta-level issues
with trying to formulate such an abstract philosophy, that's not the same as
_doing the thing_. Discussing all the issues with creating a meta-level
solution for _doing the thing_ is not the same as _doing the thing_,
interestingly enough.

I don't think it's a requirement that you should be expected to "hold the torch",
so to speak, in order to truly engage with something. Because you could obviously
be diving into something for the 'wrong reasons', but think you had the right
reasons. Your search could lead you to conclude that you didn't want to do it
and it wasted your time. Sometimes the search itself is the journey and you
upgraded your thinking, in an unquantifiable way. Not every math major wants
to become a pure mathematician.

But it's a very strong heuristic. To actually _create_. And I noticed that this
is different from _creativity_ - I can come up with non-obvious observations in
a variety of ways - open or closed - but actually _instantiating_ that into
a longer term, higher-order goal, is hard.
