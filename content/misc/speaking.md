---
title: Conversation
date: 2026-09-05
---

For all my adoration of Rorty, and how the philosophy of science took
a "practice turn", I _completely_ suck at conversation. So much for thinking
about the human element.

Part of it is probably a base intelligence. Sometimes in the conversation, I
simply cannot keep up, and sometimes I cannot express myself fully. Given the
nature of my job and the jobs I aspire to get to, this will happen.

I honestly feel like I'm getting better at the raw social aspect. I'm not
annoyed to ask people (not just friends - complete strangers, interviewers,
etc.) to repeat what they said, often because I have an ill-formed qualm, and
a decent amount of time, I'm able to articulate said qualm and we're able to
arrive at a conclusion. It's not completely second nature, but I'm surprised at
how developed that skill is.

Granted, sometimes it isn't a social aspect - if you're unable to come up with
interesting thoughts, well, too bad, you can't turn shit to gold. That happens
to me often as well - and in certain situations, you aren't allowed to just
disengage and ruminate.

---

But I don't think it's so binary. I think for me, the issue is that I don't
have much experience trying to understand someone else live, in conversation,
funnily enough. A lot of the time:

- we exchange pointers, to then look up offline
- we brush by it
- we just understand it

It's funny because I criticize things such as Solomonoff induction for allowing
arbitrarily expressive models, yet when I'm a lone duck and forced to confront
the beast, I have no real protocol. My mind goes blank. I don't try and sit
back and have any kind of protocol for mapping out the space.

And it's not like I need this _one good rule_ for doing anything. It's just
that I get stuck a bit, far too often.

---

When alone, I have remedies. One is private writing. Rumination counts too, I
guess (but not really).

I don't know. Thinking is hard, I suppose.
