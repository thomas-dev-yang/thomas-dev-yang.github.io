---
title: Liberal Ironist
date: 2026-09-02
---

The liberal ironist and the Underground Man actually have quite similar
failure modes, now that I think about it.

Well, the Underground Man is more of a, "I know you know I know you know..."
type of guy, predicting every possible reubttal and trying to 'dominate' the
conversation in some sense (which is ironic since he's just talking to himself
the entire time in his ramblings).

The liberal ironist is patronizing - publicly, they speak about absolute truths
and such, but internally, privately, they explore not just the idea of
contingency, but recursive contingency, and down the rabbithole they go.

Now that I think about it further, I don't think the actual _action_ you're
taking in the moment is much different from say, the type of thinking you
might take when you're trying to get "Cartesian certainty". Even though the
goals are diametrically opposed, the liberal ironist goes through very similar
motions in practice. Trying to achieve a design, and searching the space for
constant counterexamples to imperfect designs, are one side of the same coin -
it's just whether or not you believe that end goal is real or not; either you
target the end goal, or you believe in the process.

---

Ultimately though, I think these kinds of mindsets are poisonous. I think the
Underground Man is less poisonous than Rorty, funnily enough - but it's less
acitonable. Ultimately, even in the most charitable case, it's a battle between
people who have increasingly and increasingly sophisticated systems of how the
world "should" work, yet necessarily quotienting out things _they_ think are
useless and yet ultimately causing some type of harm.

Rorty has an interesting approach insofar as we should get rid of the final
"shoulds" in vocabulary, but (and this is the reason I framed it as "shoulds")
imposes constant engagement as the solution. "Pragmatist" in the sense of
action preceding representation/foundations, but not really a practical
solution.
