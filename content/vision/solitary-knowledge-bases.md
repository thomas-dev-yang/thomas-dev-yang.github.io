---
title: Solitary Knowledge Bases
date: 2026-08-03
---

The issue with thinking of Knowledge as Action, is that there isn't something
called, "Knowledge for the sake of pure Knowledge" anymore.

If we're to be a bit more extreme, I'll adopt a line of thinking from Sellars
and Davidson. There _is_ no neutral thing such as "knowledge for the sake of
knowledge", from which actions _then_ happen. You _always_ have an active
action frame that dictates what matters and what doesn't, in a way that imposes
real consequences on the world.

> _beacons_.

One big reason for my retreating to STEM is precisely because I thought that
the ambiguity and dependence on social and material capital in the real world
was too "accidental". We further reify this intuition with this word called
"passion", and boom, it's a nasty ball of what is "right" versus "wrong". It's
this weird desire to be decoupled as much as possible from possible barriers
(e.g. I don't want to be subject to _needing_ other people to supplement my
knowledge, for example).

The sort of "obvious" response is: well, duh, the real world is dependent on
all of these accidentals, and you're going to have to live with it. I think
this is the optimal, rational response.

I want to explore: what if we tried to make a _solitary knowledge base_? Is
this even coherent? If so, how can you enforce this properly, without flailing
around?

I don't have an answer to this. I've thought about this concrete problem for a
few days now and have no response. I keep on running up against the dichotomy
of this abstract fiction called "passion" and the weirdly pragmatic inversion
of the real world, real people, and real action mattering. As opposed to this
thing called "real knowledge".

I _think_ there is something here by dismissing the foundationalism required
for "real knowledge", yet not letting it collapse all into _action_ and
_consequences_. I mean, this is my blog, and I want to kind of stay alone here.
But I don't know what I want.

A start is "solitary knowledge bases". I like the word "solitary" here, because
in real knowledge bases, many practices are explicitly engineered around other
people (an example in science: shared standards of reproducibility. an example
in business: ownership, auditable processes). I think I want to minimize the
dependency on other people as much as possible - where "people" has a wishy
washy definition that needs to be explored more.
