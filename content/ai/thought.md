It's kind of near impossible to think about "thought" generically and make it
actionable. On my blog, I have a lot of thoughts about philosophy, and they've
definitely helped _some_ of my meta-thinking. But they aren't really that
actionable, and especially cannot contribute to any modern AI architectures.

I've been spinning on this for a while, because I'm not sure what I actually
_like_ about AI. I don't have any ideas for interpreability research, and AI
inference stuff, while cool in the abstract, in practice feels like "implement
this thing that someone else already specified on different hardware". The
interesting algorithms come from the design (as the optimization part seems well
understood as an engineering problem - maybe I would enjoy the feedback loop
part of it). But to understand the design, you have to have opinions on
intelligence. MLA, or HCA are architectural decisions that require you to both
have an intuition for what "intelligence" matters in models, and empirically
test _knowledge performance_ on real tasks. Anyone can argue that their scheme
compresses, but what matters (and will probably only ever matter for this field,
given how little we still understand about intelligence) is empirical research.

AI is super cool for building software though, and learning in general. I think
part of me inherently is just adverse to statefulness and resource requirements,
even though to do interesting stuff in the real world, you often need to build
out proper infrastructure.
